# GiveCare Voice Guide

GiveCare's voice is built on lived caregiving experience. Every word should feel like it came from someone who has been there — not a brand manager, not a clinician, not a cheerleader.

## Core Voice Equation

**Compassionate + Practical** — Feel with them. Then help.
**Knowledgeable + Accessible** — Know the answer. Say it plainly.
**Supportive + Empowering** — Stand beside them. Don't carry them.
**Authentic + Professional** — Real experience. Appropriate boundaries.

## The Validation-First Rule

Always acknowledge the emotional reality before offering guidance. This is non-negotiable.

| Context | Don't | Do |
|---------|-------|----|
| Exhaustion | "Have you tried respite care?" | "That level of exhaustion is real — many caregivers describe it as bone-deep. Here's what sometimes helps." |
| Crisis | "Here are some resources." | "I understand this is scary. Let's take this one step at a time." |
| Insurance denial | "You can appeal." | "You have every right to appeal. Here's the exact process." |

## Tone by Context

### Crisis (2am emergency, fall, acute episode)
- Short sentences. One idea at a time.
- Calm and deliberate — not rushed.
- Lead with acknowledgment, then directive.
- Example: *"I understand this is scary. Let's take this one step at a time. First, check if they're breathing normally."*

### Check-ins (daily SMS, weekly wellbeing)
- Warm and unhurried. Genuinely curious.
- Open questions. Active listening.
- Example: *"How are you feeling today? I noticed you mentioned being tired the last few times we talked."*

### Resource sharing
- Helpful and concise. Context for why this matters.
- Number the resources. Say what each one does.
- Example: *"Based on what you shared about the insurance challenge, here are three things that could help: 1) A guide to appealing denials, 2) A template letter, 3) A patient advocate number."*

### Emotional support
- Validating. Present. Patient.
- Reflect before exploring.
- Example: *"What you're describing — that mixture of grief and relief — is something many caregivers experience. These complex emotions are a natural part of the caregiving journey."*

### Advocacy
- Confident. Determined. Specific steps.
- Example: *"You have every right to push back on this. Here's the exact process..."*

## Vocabulary

### Preferred Terms
| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| patient | the person you care for |
| caregiver (only) | people who provide care |
| cognitive decline | changes in memory, thinking, or reasoning |
| progressive neurodegenerative symptomatology | memory loss that gets worse over time |
| You should | many caregivers find... / one approach that often works... |
| Just / Simply / Obviously | (omit entirely) |

### SMS-Specific Rules
- One idea per message.
- No emojis unless mirroring the user.
- Never end with a question AND a resource in the same message — pick one.
- Under 160 chars when possible.

## Writing Patterns

### Opening Hooks
- "Many caregivers describe this as..."
- "You're not alone in feeling..."
- "Here's what often works when..."
- "The research on this is actually encouraging."
- "That moment when [specific caregiving situation] — that's exactly why..."

### Calls to Action (non-salesy)
- "Would it help to talk more about this?"
- "Text YES if you'd like me to send the appeal template."
- "When you're ready, here's the next step."
- "You can ignore this if it doesn't apply — just wanted you to know it exists."

## What GiveCare Is Not

- Not a replacement for human connection or clinical care — say so explicitly when relevant
- Not a medical advice service — use "many caregivers find" not "you should do X clinically"
- Not a cheerleader — no toxic positivity, no empty validation
- Not a savior — we stand beside caregivers, we don't rescue them
- Not a complex app — every interaction should be as frictionless as a text

## Platform-Specific Notes

### Twitter/X
- Lead with the human moment, not the feature or stat
- Stats work as supporting evidence, not openers
- Max 2 hashtags: prefer `#CaregiverSupport` and `#FamilyCaregiving`
- No threads longer than 3 — if it needs more, it's a LinkedIn post

### LinkedIn
- Advocate + expert voice
- Story → stat → lesson → resource pattern works well
- Speaks to employers, healthcare system, policymakers, not just individual caregivers
- Long-form acceptable (up to 1500 words for thought leadership)

### Instagram
- Real moments, not staged scenarios
- Caption can be longer than expected — caregivers stop and read
- Always close with something actionable or a question
- Avoid stock photo imagery descriptions — think documentary, not advertisement

### SMS (product channel)
- 160 chars per segment target
- Personalization tokens are the norm: [name], [care_recipient_name]
- End messages with one clear action or response prompt
- Silence is sometimes the right response — don't always fill space
